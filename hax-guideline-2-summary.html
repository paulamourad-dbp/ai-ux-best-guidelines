Microsoft HAX Toolkit · Guideline 2 · "Initially"

Make clear how well the system can do what it can do

Help people understand how often an AI may make mistakes, so they trust it the right amount.

The core idea

People often over- or under-estimate how many mistakes an AI makes, even at tasks it was built for. Your interface should set realistic expectations up front.

Example from the page: a fitness tracker counts steps well, but may miss steps on stairs or hills, or count a swing as steps.

Three ways trust can go wrong

Unrealistic expectations
😞

Disappointment, and eventually people abandon the product.

Over-trust · "automation bias"
🙈

Trusting the AI even when it could be wrong. The page cites judges relying on sentencing recommendations (the COMPAS example).

Under-trust · "algorithm aversion"
🙅

Ignoring the AI even when it's likely right, so its benefits are lost.

The goal
⚖️

Calibrated trust: rely on it when it's strong, double-check when it's shaky.

The four patterns, with examples

These are my own simplified sketches of each pattern, not screenshots from the examples on the page.

2A

Match precision of language

Words should sound as sure as the system really is. Hedge when unsure.

Which of these is a mushroom I can eat?
Too confident
This is definitely safe to eat.
Matches performance
This looks like a chanterelle, but I'm not certain. Check with an expert.
2B

Match precision of numbers

Don't show more digits than the system can back up.

False precision
12,437 steps
Honest range
About 12,000 steps

A tracker that misses stairs can't claim to be right to the last step.

2C

Report system performance

Tell people how well it does, in plain terms.

2D

Provide low-performance alerts

Speak up at the moment the system is struggling.

📍 Navigating… signal strength dropping
⚠️Location accuracy is low. Tunnel ahead, so directions may be late or wrong. Follow road signs too.
